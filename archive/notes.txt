Yes. Memorizing the EPA Section 608 required levels of evacuation is one of the highest-yield strategies for passing the Type II and Type III sections. [1, 2] 
The EPA divides these mandates based on appliance pressure categories, the total weight of the refrigerant charge, and whether the recovery equipment used is pre-1993 or post-1993. [3, 4] 
Because modern field work and modern test questions assume the use of post-1993 equipment, focus your memory efforts on the Post-1993 column. [5]

(Measured in Inches of Mercury Vacuum / "Hg Vacuum, relative to a standard atmospheric pressure of 29.9" Hg) [3] 

| Appliance Pressure / Type | Refrigerant Examples | Full Charge Weight | Pre-Nov 15, 1993 Equipment | Post-Nov 15, 1993 Equipment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very High Pressure | R-744 (CO₂), R-410A, R-502 | Any Weight | 0" Hg (0 psig) | 0" Hg (0 psig) |
| High Pressure | R-22, R-404A, R-407C | < 200 lbs | 0" Hg (0 psig) | 0" Hg (0 psig) |

## 🧠 High-Yield Testing Traps & Hacks

* 
* The Low-Pressure Unit Trap: Pay strict attention to the units for Low Pressure (Type III). Pre-1993 is 25 inches Hg vacuum, but post-1993 is 25 mm Hg absolute. Test questions will try to swap "inches" and "mm" to trick you. [3, 4, 5] 
* The "Leaks" Exception: If a system has a verified, major leak that makes pulling a vacuum impossible, you are permitted to only isolate and recover the refrigerant until the system reaches 0 psig (atmospheric pressure) before opening it for repair. [6, 7] 
* Small Appliances (Type I Exceptions): For hermetically sealed factory systems containing 5 lbs or less of refrigerant, the vacuum metrics change. If using a post-1993 recovery machine:
* 90% recovery if the appliance compressor works.
   * 80% recovery if the compressor is dead.
   * Alternatively, pulling a 4" Hg vacuum satisfies the mandate. [4, 8, 9, 10] 
* Dehydration vs. Recovery: Do not confuse Recovery Vacuum Requirements with System Dehydration (Evacuation). The EPA recovery levels above are for pulling out refrigerant before open-air repairs. Pulling a vacuum to remove deep moisture from a system prior to a fresh charge requires dropping down to 500 microns. [5, 11, 12]
